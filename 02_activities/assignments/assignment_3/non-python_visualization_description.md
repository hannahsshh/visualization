    > What software did you use to create your data visualization?

    GraphPad Prism

    > Who is your intended audience?

    Public sector employees/city planning/government workers
    
    > What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization? 

    To show which areas of Toronto (defined by FSA) had the highest number of dog registrations in 2025 and how many registrations they had.
    
    > What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots? 

    I considered axis labels, colour, and contrast in designing the visualization. I included clear labels for both axes, as well as a title for the whole graph. I also picked an overall graph format (bar graph) that most effectively allowed me to convey my primary objective (which areas had the highest number of dog registrations and how many registrations they had). Given the intended use, I used minimal colour and straightforward design to eliminate distraction.
    
    > How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization? 

    The visualizations are reproducible because all documentation for the data and system for visualization is available. In the "Assignment-3" folder, there is the excel file "non-python_visualization_data.xlsx" which contains the original data from the Open Data site and the pivot table that was used to sort the FSAs with the highest dog registrations in 2025. There is also the GraphPad Prism file "assignment3_alternative_visualization.prism" which contains the data and the linked visualization. The original data source is also included within the figure itself so that anyone who sees the visualization knows exactly where to access the original data. 
    
    > How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible?  

    - Using colourblind-accessibility colour scheme for the colour of the bars 
    - Using high contrast black-and-white color scheme
    - Using Helvetica font for all text, which has been identified as easier to read by people with dyslexia
    - Single typeface throughout to reduce the chance of overwhelming the reader
    
    > Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?  
    
    I designed the visualization as a means of conveying information to individuals involved in decision-making towards resource allocation related to dogs, such as dog-park space or waste bins. This visualization may therefore impact all residents of these areas, if decisions are made to increase services/space in these areas, as well as those residents in areas not in the top 10 (per the data), as services/resources may be prioritized to areas with more dogs.

    > How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization? 

    I chose not to include ID numbers and breeds from the visualization because they were not relevant to the message I was trying to convey. I also chose to present data from the most recent year that had the complete dataset for the year and only to present data for dogs. My goal was for the visualization to be informative to those who may be involved in decision-making towards resource allocation related to dogs, such as dog-park space or waste bins. Cats have different needs from a public service perspective and I therefore chose to focus only on dogs.
