For each visualization, describe and justify: 

Link to the dataset: https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/licensed-dogs-and-cats/ 

    > What software did you use to create your data visualization?

    Python

    > Who is your intended audience?

    General public 
    
    > What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization? 

    To show how the total number of registered animals changes over time, and how the number of registered dogs and cats is different
    
    > What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots? 

    I considered legends, axis labels, colour, and contrast in designing the visualization. I included a legend and clear labels for both axes, as well as a title for the whole graph. I also picked an overall graph format (grouped bars) that most effectively allowed me to convey my primary objectives (total registrations by year and the comparison between dogs vs. cats). I considered high contrast colours and bar borders to make it easier to differentiate groups. 
    
    > How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization? 

    The visualization is reproducible because all the code is accessible and commented appropriately to allow for reproducibility. The original data source is also included within the figure itself so that anyone who sees the visualization knows exactly where to access the original data. The source of the data from Open Data Toronto is also updated over time, so the graph contains the information of when the included data was most recently updated.
    
    > How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible?  

    - Using colourblind-accessibility colour scheme for the colour of the bars 
    - Using borders around bars to enhance contrast
    - Using standard (serif) fonts for labels to reduce cognitive burden
    
    > Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?  
    
    The information presented is designed to be for a general public audience. It may perhaps reflect differently on people who choose to have cats vs. dogs, or change opinion on people who do not register their animals with the city. It may also impact general perspectives toward animals in Toronto, and services related to getting animals (e.g. public animal shelters).

    > How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization? 

    I chose not to include FSA, ID number, and breeds from the visualization because they were not relevant to the message I was trying to convey. My goal was to represent the number of registrations of dogs and cats by year, and to allow for comparison between the number of registrations between dogs vs. cats. The other features of the dataset were not relevant to this objective and would have therefore detracted from the message of the visualization.
