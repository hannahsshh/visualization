    - How could this data visualization have been improved?  
    ```
    Despite the helpful information conveyed about the methodology of the survey within the visualization, it is unclear how the news sources were selected – for example, if they are the most popular by viewership. While the visualization is generally clear and sparse, the number of news sources included is large and may obscure the reader from getting the most important takeaways, as the volume of repeated bars is not concise and increases the cognitive burden. Defining the type of news source (i.e. TV channel, online publication, or podcast) by colour or symbol with a relevant legend would provide additional context – as it stands, there is not differentiator and all are perceptively equal. 
    
    For accessibility and clarity, it would be useful to use an alternative colour scheme with enhanced contrast between the bars and the indicator of means, in alignment with colourblind accessible matches. A single line representing the mean would convey that the mean applies across the entirety of the visualization better, rather than individual dots.

     There are a number of reasons why the visualization is not effective, primarily because the design of the figure obscures its purpose - which is comparison.     - How could this data visualization have been improved?  
      ```
      The visualization would benefit significantly from the inclusion of x and y axes, which would help guide the reader in understanding the comparison and reduce cognitive load. A line graph that includes both global and U.S. values on the same y axes may also be obscuring the primary purpose of the graph, which is to identify the y value at the year 2015 as the largest number of cuts since 2019. The current use of lines does not clearly define that relationship. Splitting the global vs. U.S. data, and/or using an alternative layout, for example a bar chart with a trendline, may make this relationship more obvious to the reader.
